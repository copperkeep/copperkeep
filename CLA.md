# Contributor Licence Agreement

Copperkeep is licensed AGPL-3.0. Contributing requires agreeing to this, once.

## Why this exists

It is not bureaucracy, and it is worth understanding rather than skimming.

AGPL binds everyone who *receives* the code. It does not bind the copyright holder —
which is why the project can be offered as a hosted service while a competitor who forks
it cannot do the same without publishing their changes.

That asymmetry only survives while the copyright stays consolidated. If a contribution
lands under AGPL with no agreement in place, the project becomes a *licensee* of that
contribution, and AGPL section 13 then applies to the project itself: running the
combined work as a network service would oblige it to publish everything. A single
merged pull request would foreclose that option permanently, for everyone.

This agreement keeps the doors open. It is not a claim that your work matters less.

2. **You grant a copyright licence.** You grant Jeffrey Chin a perpetual, worldwide,

3. **You grant a patent licence.** If your contribution is covered by patents you

4. **You keep your copyright.** You are granting a licence, not assigning ownership. Your

5. **It is provided as-is.** You are not expected to offer warranties or support.

## How to agree

Say so in your first pull request:

> I have read CLA.md and I agree to it.

One statement covers every contribution you make afterwards.

## If you would rather not

That is a reasonable position, and plenty of people hold it. Open an issue describing the
change instead — a well-written bug report is genuinely valuable, and costs you nothing.
